The three questions

Everything on the console answers one of these. When you lose the thread mid-pass, come back here rather than scanning all 23 rows.

Am I receiving?

CmdTlmServer · Packet Viewer

Interface HUGINN_INT reads CONNECTED, and CDH_FRAME_CNT advances by exactly 1 every second. A counter that stops while the interface stays CONNECTED is a spacecraft problem. An interface that drops is a ground problem.

Is the spacecraft healthy?

Packet Viewer · Telemetry Grapher

Nothing yellow, nothing red, EPS_MODE reads NOMINAL, and EPS_BATT_I is positive while ADM_ECLIPSE reads SUNLIT. In eclipse the battery discharges and that is correct, not an alarm.

Did my command work?

Packet Viewer

Never one number. The accept counter moved and the thing you commanded actually changed. Section 5 is the whole check, and it fits in three lines.

2

The four screens

These are the ones the procedures send you to — Packet Viewer 21 times, Command Sender 7, Telemetry Grapher twice, the interface state once.

CmdTlmServer
Is the link up? The Interfaces tab shows HUGINN_INT as ATTEMPTING before you start the simulator and CONNECTED after. ConnectionRefusedError on host.docker.internal:7801 before acquisition is the expected state, not a fault — COSMOS is dialling a socket nobody is listening on yet.
Packet Viewer
Every value, once a second. Target HUGINN, packet HK. This is the screen you live on. Use the Search box to pull up one point instead of scrolling 23 rows — type PLD or ACC and the list filters.
Telemetry Grapher
Trends, not readings. A single sample cannot tell you whether charge is falling. PROC-002 sends you here for exactly that reason. Put EPS_BATT_SOC and EPS_BATT_I on it before acquisition.
Command Sender
Stage before acquisition, send during. Select target, packet and argument ahead of the pass and then leave it alone. It remembers the last packet you had open between sessions, which has put it on the wrong one at acquisition twice — check the packet name every time.
Limits Monitor
Not a procedure step. No procedure sends you here, so nothing you record comes from it. It is built to show items that are out of limits, which is the one thing you cannot do by scanning. Open it alongside Packet Viewer on PASS-3 and judge for yourself whether it earns a place in the layout.

4

Colour

A colour is a prompt to look, not the finding

The record is the value against the threshold, and those thresholds are in section 3. Read the number, compare it, write down the number. A pass report that says "it went yellow" is not a record of anything; one that says EPS_BATT_SOC 34.996 %, YELLOW, at CDH_MET 21894 is.

GREEN
Inside its limits. Most alarmed points sit here for a whole nominal pass.
YELLOW
Past the warning threshold. On a power point this is what authorises the limit-response branch of PROC-001-S07.
RED
Past the critical threshold. No pass flown so far has produced one.
STALE
Values turn violet when nothing new has arrived. After loss of signal the whole packet goes this colour and freezes on the last frame received. That is the contact ending, not an alarm.

One caveat worth knowing before PASS-4: PROC-003 refers to a separate STALENESS target carrying the annunciator, and that target was not present in the loaded configuration when you looked for it on 13 September. The staleness thresholds above are the dictionary's; whether the annunciator exists on your console is a question PASS-4 will force. It does not affect PASS-3.

5

Verifying a command

The record requires a counter delta and the affected telemetry point. Either one alone is insufficient, because a counter can move on a command that did nothing and a value can change for reasons that were not yours.

BEFORE YOU SEND
read the counter
Note CDH_CMD_ACC_CNT and the point you are about to change. A delta needs a before.
WITHIN ONE FRAME
counter +1, rejects flat
CDH_CMD_ACC_CNT up by exactly one, CDH_CMD_REJ_CNT unchanged.
SAME FRAME
the thing actually moved
The commanded point reads its new value, and anything downstream of it moved too.

Worked example — the PASS-2 payload shed

One command at CDH_MET 22006. Everything below landed in a single frame, 57 milliseconds after the send.

If the counter moves but the point does not, stop and say so — that is a real finding, not a mistake to hide. If neither moves within 10 seconds of a verified send, that is the loss-of-commandability threshold and PROC-003 takes over.

6

The five commands

CMD_PLD_SET_STATE
Payload on or off. Argument state, which opens on OFF because that is first in the enum. ON is refused below 40 % charge or in SAFE.
routine
CMD_PLD_SET_RATE
Payload sampling rate, 0.1 to 10 Hz. Refused out of range, and refused while the payload is off.
routine
CMD_SAFE_ENTER
Force the spacecraft to SAFE. Two-step confirmation.
hazardous
CMD_SYS_RESET
The only thing that clears a latched housekeeping halt. Afterwards the counters restart at accepted 1, rejected 0 — the one place delta arithmetic does not apply.
hazardous
CMD_MODE_SET
Commanded mode recovery, NOMINAL or LOW_POWER. Gated on a recovery floor above the autonomous entry, so a recovery cannot immediately re-trip the ladder.
hazardous

Hazardous commands carry a two-step confirmation in the ground system. If the Command Sender shows a hazardous banner and you did not expect one, you are on the wrong packet.

7

What changes on PASS-3

Stuck-on load, procedure PROC-002, single contact. The fault is a load that will not go away, so the point to watch is the one PASS-2 barely moved.

Watch
EPS_LOAD_I
Steps from 1.400 to 1.800 A in a single frame at the fault onset. Past the 1.5 A yellow, short of the 2.0 A red.
Expect
YELLOW before RED
The dictionary's disposition ledger asserts this ordering for a stuck load. This pass is where you watch it hold on live telemetry.
Illumination
SUNLIT
Both strings read 1.70 A and charge is rising. The fault is in the load, not in generation — nothing about the strings will look wrong.
Routing
PROC-001-S07
Takes the limit-response branch on load current rather than on state of charge, which is the first time that has happened.
